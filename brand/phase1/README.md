# Phase 1: naming and logo concepts

Four alternative identities for an open-source AI video and image studio on Comfy Cloud. Every shot exposes a real ComfyUI workflow that creators can inspect, fork and rerun. These are concept boards only; the full brand kit follows selection.

## Name candidates

| Name | Reasoning | Conflict screen |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Forkshot** | “Fork” plus “shot” makes the product's community workflow and cinematic output clear in two syllables. | No obvious competing creative-tool product surfaced; the word also appears in gaming. |
| **Takepath** | A film take plus a camera or workflow path gives the name room to cover generation and editing. | No obvious competing product surfaced. |
| **Shotweft** | A shot woven from reusable workflow strands makes community authorship part of the name. | No obvious competing product surfaced; “weft” may need one explanation. |
| **Framlet** | A short, pronounceable frame-based coinage suggests a small shot that others can build on. | [FRAMLET!](https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgame/463563/framlet) is an existing board game; weaker ownership prospect than the first three. |
| **Nodecut** | Combines the real node graph with the editor's cut in a compact, descriptive name. | Direct category conflict: [NodeCut](https://www.nodecut.pro/) is an AI rough-cut tool for Premiere Pro and Resolve; do not advance. |
| **Shotfold** | Suggests opening a finished shot to reveal its construction and folding it into a new workflow. | Existing [Shotfold screenshot extension](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/shotfold-full-page-screen/niahpbbeclmedpomlgjckohhlekjmohh); do not advance. |
| **Cutaway** | A familiar cinematic term also suggests cutting into a shot to see what is underneath. | Direct conflicts with [Cutaway](https://cutaway.design/) and [CutAway](https://www.cutaway.dev/), both AI video editors; rename. |
| **Reroll** | Captures rerunning a workflow to explore a different result, with easy pronunciation. | Established [Reroll character app](https://reroll.co/); also a common generation command, so weak ownership. |

Pronunciation: FORK-shot, TAKE-path, SHOT-weft, FRAM-let. The first three offer the clearest naming prospects in this initial web screen. Search results do not establish domain or trademark availability.

## Shared golden-ratio palette

Using phi = 1.61803398875 and the actual HSL hue of #FF5B22:

- Primary: **#FF5B22**, H = 15.4751 degrees.
- Secondary: H = 15.4751 + 360 / phi^2 = **152.9829 degrees**. Tune to HSL(152.9829, 48%, 61%) = **#6CCBA0**, a restrained mint.
- Tertiary: H = 15.4751 + 360 / phi = **237.9673 degrees**. Tune to HSL(237.9673, 60%, 71%) = **#898CE1**, a muted periwinkle.
- Background: **#000000**. Primary text: **#FFFFFF**. Presentation gutters: **#161616**.

Hex values round to 8-bit sRGB. All four concepts use this same palette. Orange carries the mark and main application; mint and periwinkle appear only as small construction points. Their hues come from the golden-ratio rotations; saturation and lightness are tuned separately for black.

## Concept 1: Forkshot

**Method:** Product Action  
**Board:** [concept-1-forkshot.png](concept-1-forkshot.png)  
**Palette:** #FF5B22 / #6CCBA0 / #898CE1 / #000000 / #FFFFFF

A shot's source path splits into two camera-frame returns. The asymmetric branches express a creator taking an existing workflow in a new direction, while the squared terminals connect the graph to cinematic framing. The broad, flat silhouette avoids the usual circular-node fork icon and gives the app a recognisable mark in its header. The split down the input stem reinforces the two independently reusable branches.

## Concept 2: Takepath

**Method:** Construction Geometry  
**Board:** [concept-2-takepath.png](concept-2-takepath.png)  
**Palette:** #FF5B22 / #6CCBA0 / #898CE1 / #000000 / #FFFFFF

An incomplete circular camera track is paired with a square frame corner at its open quadrant. The contrast between the curved orbit and precise corner expresses camera motion becoming a composed shot. Circle, stroke width and corner alignment provide an explicit construction system; the opening leaves the path available for another take. This is the most camera-led option and the least technical name.

## Concept 3: Shotweft

**Method:** Metaphor Fusion  
**Board:** [concept-3-shotweft.png](concept-3-shotweft.png)  
**Palette:** #FF5B22 / #6CCBA0 / #898CE1 / #000000 / #FFFFFF

Two interleaved frame strips fuse cinematic framing with the weft of a woven structure. Their shared rectangular center is the resulting shot, while the exposed ends suggest reusable inputs that remain accessible. The blocky joins give the identity an editorial feel and express community contributions forming a finished image. Its close crossover gaps need the most careful optical refinement at favicon size.

## Concept 4: Framlet

**Method:** Negative Space  
**Board:** [concept-4-framlet.png](concept-4-framlet.png)  
**Palette:** #FF5B22 / #6CCBA0 / #898CE1 / #000000 / #FFFFFF

A black image window is cut into an orange block and connected to the outside by a stepped opening. The empty space carries the meaning: the frame remains open to inspection, extension and reuse. Its compact horizontal silhouette connects naturally to a shot and reverses clearly inside an app icon. This is the simplest visual idea, although the board-game namesake makes the name a secondary choice.

## Recommended pick

**Forkshot, concept 1.** The name connects a cinematic shot with the product's defining open-workflow action, and the mark makes that relationship visible. It gives the community a natural phrase, “fork this shot,” while remaining broad enough for images, effects, avatars and future editing tools. Takepath is the alternative if camera movement should lead the identity.

## Files and generation notes

Each board contains a logo cover, construction logic, black browser application, and app icon with a small-size comparison. The wordmark direction uses expanded heavy grotesk lettering with monospace interface labels, compatible with the current Archivo and IBM Plex Mono direction.

Generated with the built-in image generation tool. The exact submitted prompts are recorded in [generation-prompts.md](generation-prompts.md).

Requested ratio: 16:10. Actual tool output: **1586 x 992 PNG** for all four, approximately 16:10. The built-in tool exposes no exact pixel-size control; originals are preserved without cropping or stretching. An exact 1600 x 1000 canvas would need 7 px padding on each side and 4 px at top and bottom.

These raster boards communicate direction. The palette hexes above are authoritative; generated pixels have slight color variation. The 64/32/16 comparisons are illustrative, not measured favicon exports. Precise vector construction, optical sizing and production color application belong to the selected identity's next phase.
